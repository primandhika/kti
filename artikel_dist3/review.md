## Summary
This draft presents a quasi-experimental mixed-method study of **Bicaranta**, a microlearning-based multimedia platform that combines short modules, explain-back tasks, self-recording, playback, and reflection. The paper’s intended contribution is stronger than a generic “technology helps speaking” claim: it tries to show that platform-supported speaking practice can improve both **speaking performance** and **metacognitive awareness**, and it offers qualitative mechanisms for why that might happen.

Relative to **Mahdi (2022)**, the draft is conceptually more focused: it has a clearer pedagogical mechanism (microlearning + Feynman-style explain-back + reflection) and is more cautious about causal overclaiming. However, **Mahdi (2022) is rhetorically and procedurally more complete as a journal article**: it specifies intervention dosage, participant profile, instrument procedures, inter-rater reliability, and standard end matter (funding/conflict statements, appendix material). Against **current CALL-EJ expectations**, this draft is **not yet submission-ready**. The biggest blockers are incomplete manuscript metadata, under-specified methods, reliance on analysis notes rather than a clean rerun, and unresolved measurement/rater issues. In its current form, it is closer to a strong internal draft than to a paper that can survive CALL-EJ’s initial editorial screening.

## Strengths
- [S1] **Strong topical fit for CALL-EJ.** A technology-supported speaking intervention is clearly within the journal’s scope, and recent CALL-EJ papers continue to publish empirical studies on speaking, AI tools, metaverse/blended environments, and related affective variables.
- [S2] **Better conceptual mechanism than the benchmark.** Compared with Mahdi (2022), this draft more clearly explains *how* the platform may work: chunked content, explain-back, playback, and reflection are tied to planning and self-monitoring rather than just “using multimedia.”
- [S3] **Useful inclusion of metacognitive awareness.** This gives the paper a more interesting angle than a simple pre/post speaking-score report and could differentiate it from many CALL speaking papers if the measure is properly documented and validated.
- [S4] **Healthy skepticism in the discussion.** The manuscript explicitly acknowledges quasi-experimental limits, possible contamination, rater issues, and weak questionnaire reliability. That honesty is a real strength.
- [S5] **Some CALL-EJ compliance is already in place.** The abstract is within the stated 200-word limit, and the keyword count is already appropriate.

## Weaknesses
- [W1] **FATAL: The manuscript is not yet a submission-quality artifact.** It still contains an internal drafting note, multiple placeholders, incomplete author metadata, unfinished acknowledgments/biodata, and appears to be only about **4.4k words**, which is well below CALL-EJ’s stated **6,000-8,000-word** full-article range. This alone makes the current file unsuitable for submission.
- [W2] **FATAL: The methods section is substantially under-specified relative to both Mahdi (2022) and current CALL-EJ expectations.** The paper does not clearly report intervention duration, number of sessions/modules, task sequence, teacher role, class equivalence, sampling logic for interviews, ethics/consent procedures, or enough instrument detail for replication. Mahdi (2022), despite its own limitations, is more complete on these basics.
- [W3] **MAJOR: The quantitative analysis is not publication-grade yet.** The manuscript openly states that inferential results were inherited from project notes and should be rerun. That means the current inferential claims are not fully auditable from the paper itself. In addition, the design uses intact classes, possible control contamination, and baseline differences in metacognitive scores, but the analysis as written still leans on gain-based comparisons rather than a clearly justified primary model such as ANCOVA or a mixed model.
- [W4] **MAJOR: Measurement quality is a serious risk, especially for the questionnaire and speaking assessment.** The paper reports negative Cronbach alpha values for some questionnaire scales, which undermines those scales as evidence. At the same time, the speaking-rater procedure is too thinly documented: no rater count, blinding, training, agreement statistics, or rubric provenance are provided in the manuscript.
- [W5] **MAJOR: Several results claims outrun the evidence actually shown.** Aspect-level speaking claims are made without an aspect-level table. Mechanism claims from qualitative data are plausible, but the sampling and coding process are too vague to support strong mixed-method inference. The paper says “mixed-method” and “R&D-informed,” but neither design is operationalized rigorously enough.
- [W6] **MAJOR: The journal positioning is promising but still under-argued.** The draft gestures toward a gap, but it does not yet sharply explain why this paper is the right paper for CALL-EJ *now*, given the journal’s recent publication of AI, metaverse, e-portfolio, and other technology-mediated speaking studies. The “lower-threshold/scalable CALL design” angle is potentially strong, but it needs to be foregrounded and defended with better related-work positioning.
- [W7] **MINOR: Reference and editorial cleanup remain incomplete.** At least one reference still has a missing page range, and the manuscript still includes boilerplate placeholders that should never appear in a submitted file.

## Questions for Authors
- [Q1] What was the **exact intervention dosage** in the field test: number of weeks, number of sessions, module sequence, expected time-on-task, and whether both groups had the same instructor and assignments?
- [Q2] How were the **speaking scores produced**: how many raters, were they blinded to condition/time, what training was used, what was the inter-rater reliability, and was the rubric adapted from an existing validated source?
- [Q3] What are the **psychometric details** of the metacognitive instrument and post-use questionnaire: item counts, response scale, score range, validation evidence, and why did some questionnaire subscales yield negative alpha values?
- [Q4] If intact classes were used and **some control students may have accessed the platform**, how will the final analysis handle contamination and preserve a credible treatment comparison?
- [Q5] Was the study reviewed by an **ethics committee**, and how were participant consent, recording permission, and data handling managed?

## Verdict
**Overall assessment:** promising topic, plausible CALL-EJ fit, but **not submission-ready**.

If submitted in its current form, I would expect either a **desk rejection at internal screening** or a **major-revision outcome before meaningful external review**, mainly because the paper is incomplete as an artifact and not yet methodologically auditable. The good news is that the core study may still be publishable: the topic fits the journal, the dataset appears nontrivial, and the mechanism is more interesting than the benchmark paper’s broader multimedia claim. But the paper needs a serious finalization pass, not just light copyediting.

**Would this pass at CALL-EJ now?** Probably **no**.

**Confidence:** 0.88

## Revision Plan
1. **Make the manuscript submittable first** ([W1], [W7]).
   - Delete the drafting note.
   - Fill in affiliation, email, ORCID, acknowledgments, biodata, and any required declarations.
   - Bring the paper into the journal’s expected **6,000-8,000-word** range by adding the missing methodological and analytical detail rather than padding the discussion.
   - Run a full APA 7 cleanup of references and headings.

2. **Freeze the analytic dataset and rerun all statistics from raw data** ([W1], [W3], [W4]).
   - Do not submit a paper that says inferential values came from prior notes.
   - Choose and justify a primary model: preferably **posttest ~ group + pretest** (ANCOVA/regression) or a mixed model if repeated measures are retained.
   - Report exact test choice, assumptions, effect sizes, confidence intervals, and how missing/paired data were handled.
   - Rebuild every numerical table directly from the final script/notebook and cross-check line by line.

3. **Rewrite the Methods section for replication** ([W2], [Q1]-[Q5]).
   - Add course context, student level, age/semester if available, class assignment logic, number of instructors, intervention timeline, session count, module structure, assessment timing, and control condition equivalence.
   - State whether the design is best described as **quasi-experimental mixed methods**, **developmental evaluation**, or **R&D**; if you keep “R&D-informed,” specify the development model and what expert review/trial criteria were used.
   - Add ethics approval/consent/recording permission details.

4. **Strengthen instrument reporting and either repair or downgrade weak measures** ([W4]).
   - For speaking: give the full rubric, score range, weighting, rater training, rater count, and reliability/agreement statistic.
   - For metacognition: identify the instrument source/adaptation, item count, construct dimensions, sample item(s), and validation evidence.
   - For the post-use questionnaire: if some subscales have negative alpha, either (a) re-specify/recompute the scale structure properly, or (b) remove scale-based interpretation and use the questionnaire only as descriptive/qualitative support.

5. **Rebuild the Results section so every claim is evidenced** ([W3], [W5]).
   - Add an inferential summary table for both primary outcomes.
   - Add an **aspect-level speaking table** if you want to claim organization/language accuracy/delivery advantages.
   - Add a participant-flow description from initial recruitment to the 77 paired cases.
   - Clarify whether qualitative participants were sampled purposively, by achievement, by usage intensity, etc.

6. **Sharpen the CALL-EJ contribution and benchmark comparison** ([W6]).
   - Explicitly frame the contribution as a **scalable, lower-infrastructure CALL design** for speaking development, contrasted with heavier AI/metaverse solutions in recent CALL-EJ papers.
   - Use Mahdi (2022) as a **benchmark for rhetorical completeness**, not as the methodological ceiling.
   - Add a tighter paragraph explaining what this study contributes beyond “multimedia helps speaking”: namely, a structured mechanism for **planning, explain-back, playback, and metacognitive regulation**.

7. **Tighten the discussion and conclusion** ([W5], [W6]).
   - Keep the causal language modest.
   - Separate what the data show (improvement pattern) from what the qualitative data suggest (possible mechanisms).
   - End with implications that are specific to CALL-EJ readers: classroom integration, low-cost design, platform deployment constraints, and future comparative studies.
